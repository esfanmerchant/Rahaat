## About Rahat {#about}

Rahat is a prototype relief program, not an official service, and every payment in it is simulated.
Rahat gives a one-time relief payment to low-income households.
The department decides every application with rule version 1.

## Who can get the payment {#who-can-get}

The payment needs three things: a confirmed identity, a monthly household income of Rs 30,000 or less, and a household that does not already receive this payment.
The identity is confirmed by the signed Rahat card or by the registry.
A household with a monthly income above Rs 30,000 does not meet the income rule.

## Income limit {#income-limit}

The income limit is Rs 30,000 a month for the whole household.
The income of everyone in the home is counted together.
An income of exactly Rs 30,000 is within the limit.

## Who comes first {#priority}

Widows, households with a person with a disability, and households of five or more people get priority.
Priority helps a case, but the three main rules must still be met.

## The payment {#payment}

The relief payment is Rs 12,000, paid one time to each approved household.
The money goes to the mock wallet or account given in the application.
A payment above the department limit needs a second officer to approve it.

## How the decision is made {#decision}

When the system is not sure, a person checks the application.
The system never rejects an application only because of an answer the applicant gave about themself.
Every decision is recorded with the rule version that was used.

## If the answer to a question is not known {#unknown-answers}

"I do not know" is an allowed answer.
An answer that is not known stays empty, and nobody guesses it.
A person may ask for one more paper to settle a missing answer.
